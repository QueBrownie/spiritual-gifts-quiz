Spiritual Gifts Inventory

A self-assessment to help you notice how God may have gifted you to serve His church.

Before you start

You'll rate 64 statements — about 6–8 minutes. For each one, choose how often it is true of you.

Answer about what you actually do, not what you value or aspire to. The most useful results come from thinking of specific, recent instances. If a statement describes something you've never had the chance to do, answer "Never."

Some statements are worded in reverse, so agreeing with everything won't produce a useful profile.

What to do with this

A questionnaire can only report what you told it about yourself. Gifts are confirmed in practice and in community, not by a score. Two suggestions:

Ask two or three people who serve alongside you what they'd have picked for you. Where their answer and yours agree, pay attention. Where they differ, that gap is worth a conversation.

Then go serve in one area for a season and notice what bears fruit and what drains you. That is far better evidence than this page.

Don't treat a low score as "I don't have to do that." Every believer is called to serve, give, show mercy, and share their faith; this only suggests where you may be especially fruitful.

How this is scored (and its limits)

Reverse-worded items. One statement per gift is phrased in the opposite direction and scored backward, so answering everything the same way produces a deliberately flat — and clearly flagged — profile rather than a fake result.

Compared to your own average, not to other people. People differ in how generously they rate themselves. Rather than a raw total, each gift is shown as the distance from your own average response, in units of your own spread. So a cautious rater and a confident rater get comparable profiles.

Statistical ties are shown as ties. With four statements per gift, small differences are noise. Gifts within one standard error of your top score are reported together as a band, rather than pretending #1 beats #2.

Mixed, blind item order. Statements are shuffled so no two consecutive ones measure the same gift, and gift names are hidden during the assessment.

Limits. This is not a validated psychometric instrument. It has no population norms, so "relative" scores compare you only to yourself. Four items per gift is enough to be suggestive, not conclusive. Self-report reflects self-perception, including blind spots in both directions.

Sources

Lists of spiritual gifts appear in Romans 12:6–8; 1 Corinthians 12:8–10, 28–30; Ephesians 4:11; and 1 Peter 4:9–11.

Gift definitions adapted from Jesus on Leadership: Developing Servant Leaders by Gene Wilkes (LifeWay Christian Resources, 1998), which draws on definitions from Ken Hemphill, Serving God: Discovering and Using Your Spiritual Gifts (1995), and C. Peter Wagner, Your Spiritual Gifts Can Help Your Church Grow (Regal Books, 1979). Following that source, the "sign gifts" are not included.

The rating statements are not from the source document; they were written for this inventory from each gift's definition.
